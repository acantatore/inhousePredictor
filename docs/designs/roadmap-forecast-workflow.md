# Design: Roadmap Forecast Workflow

## Problem Statement
Internal confidence signals already exist, but they are fragmented across Slack, spreadsheets, decks, and ad hoc status updates. That makes them stale, unauditable, and hard to compare over time. The goal is to evolve InhousePredictor from a market-first product into a forecasting-first workflow for roadmap commitments, where teams can record confidence explicitly, explain why it changed, and give leaders earlier warning on delivery risk.

## Demand Evidence
- The strongest current signal is behavioral, not aspirational: teams already hack this problem together with Slack confidence updates, spreadsheet tracking, and docs/status decks.
- The pain is not “we need a new app”; it is “the company already wants confidence signals, but nobody trusts the current fragmented versions enough to reuse them.”
- This is especially acute for commitment-heavy planning and review cycles where confidence needs to be revisited, not just stated once.

## Status Quo
Today the workflow is fragmented:
- confidence gets posted in Slack, then disappears into the scrollback
- someone keeps a manual spreadsheet, which drifts from reality and lacks rationale history
- planning docs and decks contain confidence language, but it is stale and hard to audit
- postmortems cannot cleanly answer who believed what, when, or why that belief changed

Cost of the workaround:
- leaders cannot trust a single source of truth
- teams duplicate effort across tools
- confidence is usually snapshots, not history
- accountability becomes social memory instead of system memory

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
### Target User
The most desperate user is an exec managing commitments across teams. They need earlier warning, clearer accountability, and a way to compare changing confidence across roadmap items without relying on scattered Slack messages and spreadsheets.

### Narrowest Wedge
Start with one roadmap-forecast workflow:
- create a forecast question for a roadmap commitment
- let contributors submit a direct probability and rationale
- preserve append-only forecast history over time
- show how confidence moved and why
- expose one clean executive view for the official internal forecast and current risk

This is the smallest version likely to make a sponsor say, “yes, this is worth backing.”

## Constraints
- This is an internal company product, not a public startup app.
- The current system already has a working market engine, admin governance, auth, and frontend shell.
- The forecasting pivot should preserve the current calm, trustworthy tone rather than rebrand the product from scratch.
- The first step should be a wedge, not the full “forecasting platform” described in the research report.
- Existing market functionality should not be deleted immediately; it should become secondary if the pivot succeeds.

## Premises
1. The real pain is fragmented and unauditable confidence, not the absence of a prediction market.
2. The earliest internal buyer is an exec managing commitments across teams.
3. A roadmap-forecast workflow is the narrowest wedge with meaningful sponsor value.
4. The product should preserve its current trust-oriented tone.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: Roadmap Forecast Workflow
Add a forecasting-first layer focused only on roadmap commitments: direct probability input, rationale, revision history, simple Brier-based scoring, and a lightweight executive view.

Pros:
- directly matches the current pain
- smallest sponsor-visible wedge
- reuses current product shell and governance instincts

Cons:
- narrower than the full long-term report vision
- not yet a generalized forecasting platform
- external signals remain secondary at first

### Approach B: Full Forecasting Layer Pivot
Reorient the entire product around direct forecasting, rationales, scoring, notifications, cohorts, dashboards, and external signals from the start.

Pros:
- closest to the report’s complete vision
- strongest long-term conceptual coherence if successful

Cons:
- large product and architecture jump
- high implementation and adoption risk
- easy to overbuild before proving internal demand

### Approach C: Executive Risk Console
Build an exec-facing dashboard first, aggregating confidence, rationale highlights, and change-over-time for selected roadmap items.

Pros:
- fastest sponsor-facing demo
- very easy to explain internally

Cons:
- weak bottoms-up behavior change
- risks becoming another reporting layer instead of a workflow
- may improve visibility without improving truth quality

## Recommended Approach
Choose Approach A: Roadmap Forecast Workflow.

This is the cleanest wedge between the current market-first product and the report’s full forecasting vision. It solves a real, existing internal pain, gives an exec sponsor something concrete to evaluate, and reuses enough of the current system to keep the transition realistic.

### Product Shape
- `Forecast Question` becomes the canonical object for this new workflow.
- Direct probability input becomes the primary interaction.
- Rationales are first-class, attached to every meaningful forecast update.
- Forecast revisions are append-only.
- Brier score is the single official score, and in phase 1 it is tracked per contributor rather than as a separate aggregate product score.
- Calibration and coverage are derived analytics, not competing headline scores.
- External signals are read-only overlays only.
- The current market engine remains as a secondary advanced module, not the main workflow.
- Every `ForecastQuestion` automatically creates a paired `Market`, but that market is shadow signal only and never becomes the official internal forecast.

### Core Entities
- `ForecastQuestion` - one roadmap commitment being evaluated, including owner, resolution rule, open/close timestamps, and visibility
- `ForecastRevision` - append-only probability update by a contributor, including timestamp and rationale
- `ForecastProjection` - the shared current-state projection for latest active contributor forecasts, official internal forecast, and contributor snapshots at close
- `ResolutionRecord` - final outcome, evidence source, resolver, and resolution timestamp
- `ScoreRecord` - Brier score and derived analytics computed after resolution
- `ExternalSignalSnapshot` - periodic read-only comparison value from an outside source
- `BackgroundJob` - DB-backed job record for reminders, resolution notifications, and external signal snapshot work

### Roles and Permissions
- `Executive viewer` - can see the official internal forecast, movement over time, and rationale summaries
- `Commitment owner` - creates the question and proposes initial resolution rule
- `Contributor` - submits and updates forecasts with rationale
- `Resolver / committee / admin` - records the final outcome with evidence and handles disputes if needed

### Official Internal Forecast
The official internal forecast is the product’s canonical probability for a roadmap commitment. In phase 1, define it as the median of the latest active forecast from each eligible contributor on that question. External overlays never change this number. The displayed `current risk` is simply `1 - official internal forecast`.

### Lifecycle Ownership
- `ForecastQuestion` is the only writable lifecycle owner for open / close / resolve transitions.
- The paired `Market` lifecycle is derived from the `ForecastQuestion` and is never independently edited in phase 1.

### Market Relationship
- Every `ForecastQuestion` auto-creates a paired `Market`.
- The paired market exists as an advanced internal signal only.
- It never changes the official internal forecast, contributor scoring, or notification triggers.

### Aggregation Boundary
The `forecast` module owns all canonical projections:
- latest active contributor forecasts
- official internal forecast calculation
- contributor snapshot at close

Dashboards, scoring, notifications, and executive summaries must consume these shared projections instead of recomputing directly from raw revision history.

### Background Work Model
Phase 1 uses a Postgres-backed job table plus one in-app worker process for:
- reminders
- resolution notifications
- external signal snapshot ingestion

Notifications and snapshot refreshes do not run inline in request handlers.

### Happy Path Workflow
1. A commitment owner creates a forecast question with a clear resolution rule.
2. Selected contributors submit an initial probability and rationale.
3. Contributors revise forecasts over time as new evidence appears.
4. Leadership reviews the official internal forecast, rationale trend, and change-over-time view.
5. A resolver records the final outcome with evidence.
6. The system computes Brier score and derived analytics from the revision history.
7. The team uses the result in review/postmortem discussion.

### Phase 1 Executive View
The first executive view should show one program-level slice, not the whole company. It should show only:
- commitment title
- official internal forecast
- current risk (`1 - forecast`)
- change over the last 7 days
- latest 3 rationale excerpts
- resolution owner
- close / resolve date
- external overlay value if available

## Phase 1 vs Later Phases

### Phase 1
- roadmap forecast question creation
- direct probability input
- append-only revision history
- paired market auto-creation as shadow signal only
- shared forecast projection layer
- rationale capture (mandatory on create and on forecast changes of 10 percentage points or more)
- official internal forecast display
- one executive risk view
- evidence-backed resolution
- Brier score plus basic coverage/calibration analytics
- DB-backed background jobs for reminders, notifications, and snapshot refreshes

### Phase 1 Scoring Rule
- Contributor score: compute a standard Brier score for each contributor using that contributor's latest forecast before close.
- Official product display: show the official internal forecast trend separately from contributor scores; do not invent a second “aggregate score” in phase 1.
- Coverage and calibration are supporting analytics derived from contributor forecast history, not competing headline scores.
- Time boundary rule: revisions after close must never alter contributor snapshot at close, official internal forecast history at close, or Brier scoring inputs.

### Later Phases
- richer notification cadences
- cohort segmentation and private domains
- broader dashboarding across org levels
- external signal connectors beyond simple snapshots
- promises-audit automation and deeper workflow integrations

## Out of Scope for Phase 1
- full forecasting platform across every use case
- SSO and enterprise identity overhaul
- full external data warehouse / time-series platform
- broad Jira / Linear / OKR integrations
- deleting the current market engine immediately
- multiple competing score systems
- AI-generated rationale summarization
- org-wide generalized dashboards beyond one executive view

## Success Criteria
- A roadmap commitment can be turned into a forecast question with clear resolution criteria.
- Contributors can enter and revise confidence directly, with rationale preserved in history.
- An exec can open one view and understand:
  - the current official internal forecast
  - how it changed over time
  - why it changed
- The system produces one official Brier-based contributor score after resolution, with coverage/calibration shown as supporting analytics.
- At least one internal planning or review ritual changes behavior because this workflow exists.

## Dependencies
- Existing auth, frontend shell, and admin governance can be reused.
- The market engine can remain in place while forecasting-first UX is introduced.
- Future extensions depend on this wedge proving real adoption before adding cohorts, notifications breadth, and external connectors.
- The shared forecast projection layer is required before executive views, notifications, and scoring can be implemented cleanly.

## Phase 1 Governance Rules
- `Roadmap commitment` means a date-bound deliverable such as a launch milestone, milestone completion, or committed release target.
- Eligible contributors in phase 1 are a curated set chosen by the commitment owner plus one executive or program-review sponsor.
- Resolution in phase 1 is handled by a named review committee or admin-backed resolver, not by the commitment owner alone.
- Outcome set in phase 1 is binary: `delivered by date` or `not delivered by date`.
- Partial delivery is treated as `not delivered by date` unless the resolution rule explicitly defines a narrower milestone target up front.

## Feasibility Risks and Mitigations
- `Official forecast ambiguity` - risk that people do not understand how one internal number is produced; mitigate by defining aggregation explicitly before implementation starts.
- `Resolution governance confusion` - risk that teams dispute who is allowed to resolve; mitigate by selecting one clear resolution body for phase 1.
- `Contributor sprawl` - risk that too many contributors reduce signal quality; mitigate by constraining the first wedge to selected roadmap stakeholders.
- `Workflow duplication` - risk that teams keep using Slack/docs in parallel; mitigate by centering one executive-visible workflow around a real commitment that matters.
- `Overbuilding` - risk that the team drifts into full platform work too early; mitigate by treating this wedge as phase 1 and explicitly deferring the broader system.
- `Projection drift` - risk that scoring, dashboards, and notifications each compute their own truth; mitigate by requiring a shared forecast projection layer.
- `Shadow-market leakage` - risk that the paired market silently becomes authoritative; mitigate with explicit non-authoritative market tests and a hard architecture rule that direct forecasts remain canonical.
